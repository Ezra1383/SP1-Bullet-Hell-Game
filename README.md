# Rail Shooter Game With Computer Vision

A rail shooter you play with your body. No controller, no VR headset, no
keyboard. Point a webcam at yourself and you're in.

Lean to steer your spaceship. Raise your arms to aim two crosshairs at once.
The game fires for you the moment you're on target. Dodge incoming fire by
moving the way you'd move if the danger were real, because on your screen,
it is.

## The pitch

VR headsets and motion controllers cost money most players don't want to
spend for one genre. This game strips that cost out. All you need is a
webcam most laptops already have built in, and a body.

Move to the top-left corner of your camera frame and your ship moves to the
top-left corner of the screen. Raise your left arm and a crosshair follows
it. Raise your right arm and a second, independent crosshair follows that
one. Both crosshairs auto-fire the instant they land on an enemy. You don't
press a single button.

![Screenshot placeholder](screenshots/gameplay-2.png)

## How it plays

- **Move to survive.** Your body is the joystick. Duck, lean, and shift to
  dodge incoming enemy fire.
- **Aim with both hands, independently.** Left wrist and right wrist each
  drive their own crosshair, so you can cover two angles of attack at once.
- **No trigger, no cooldown timer to watch.** Line up a shot and the ship
  fires on its own.
- **Waves, not levels.** Enemies spawn for 15 seconds, then a 3-second lull
  gives you a breath before the next wave starts.
- **Score by staying alive.** Kills and dodged projectiles both count. The
  run ends when your health hits zero, not on a clock.

## What people said after playing it

We brought the game to the Senior Project Exhibition and put it in front of
42 people who had never touched it before. No tutorial video, no coach
standing over their shoulder. Just the webcam and the game.

- **Average experience rating: 8.98 out of 10.** 95% of players scored it
  an 8 or higher.
- **97.6% said they'd play again.** 52.4% said "Definitely yes."
- **78.5% called it responsive**, rating responsiveness 8 or higher.
- **64.3% noticed zero lag.** Only a small fraction reported anything they'd
  call severe.

One player summed up the learning curve: "First time, I didn't know the
rules." Give it a few waves and it clicks. Another wrote that it "became
fun to play" almost as soon as the rules landed.

## What you need to play

- A PC.
- A standard USB or built-in webcam. No depth sensor, no IR camera, no
  Kinect.
- Enough open floor space to lean, duck, and raise both arms.
- Decent room lighting. The tracking works best when the camera can see you
  clearly.

That's the whole hardware list. The build is a single executable; there's
nothing else to install to get it running.

## Where this goes next

Player feedback pointed at a short list of real improvements: a visible
signal when the camera loses tracking, calmer visual effects so incoming
enemies don't get lost in the explosions, and built-in rest beats so players
aren't holding their arms up the entire run. All three are on the roadmap.
